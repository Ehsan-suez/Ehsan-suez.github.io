My research focuses on **infectious disease modeling, epidemic forecasting, and public health analytics**. I use computational and statistical approaches to understand disease transmission, evaluate predictive models, and generate evidence that can support public health preparedness and response.

## 1. Infectious Disease Modeling and Outbreak Analytics

My current work at the **CDC Center for Forecasting and Outbreak Analytics** focuses on infectious disease modeling and outbreak analytics to inform public health decision-making. My methodological interests include branching processes, renewal models, event-driven agent-based simulation, and probabilistic programming, particularly for understanding transmission dynamics and evaluating outbreak scenarios.

## 2. Epidemic Forecasting and Model Evaluation

A central focus of my doctoral research at the **University of Georgia** was improving the accuracy and reliability of infectious disease forecasts. This work included evaluating probabilistic forecasting methods, optimizing baseline models used in collaborative forecasting hubs, and examining how modeling assumptions influence predictive performance. My research also includes **ScenarioCast**, an approach that translates long-term epidemic scenario projections into short-term influenza and COVID-19 forecasts, as well as collaborative forecasting and ensemble evaluation for respiratory diseases in the Southern Hemisphere.

## 3. Infectious Disease Nowcasting and Surveillance

I have worked on methods for estimating recent disease activity when surveillance data are incomplete or delayed. These projects include developing machine-learning approaches to adjust for reporting delays in influenza hospitalizations and surveillance-based methods for estimating the growth of emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants. This research aims to improve the timeliness and usefulness of infectious disease surveillance and situational awareness.

## 4. Applied Epidemiology and Public Health Research

Beyond infectious disease modeling, I have contributed to collaborative epidemiological research involving systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and population health outcomes. These projects span infectious diseases and other public health topics, including HIV, hepatitis B, and health outcomes among patients receiving dialysis.

Across these areas, I am interested in connecting **quantitative methods, infectious disease epidemiology, and public health practice**. For related work, see my [publications](/publications/).
